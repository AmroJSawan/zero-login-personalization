# Model Selection Guide: Anonymous Session Personalization
### Anonymous POMDP, episodic reset, 1k-100k catalog, ~30ms edge budget, 5-30 events/session

Scope note: every number below is tagged for verification status. `[V]` = verified against the primary source by the verifier pass. `[C]` = corrected from the survey (survey value was wrong; corrected value shown). `[U]` = could not be confirmed; treat as a hypothesis, not a design input. Unverified numbers are never used to justify a build decision in this document.

---

## 1. The five findings that should change your plan

**Finding 1: A 2019 nearest-neighbour heuristic is still the number to beat, and nobody in the 2022-2026 literature has tried to beat it.**

Three independent evaluations under equal tuning budgets all land the same way. Ludewig et al. (UMUAI 2021) ran 12 algorithms across 8 datasets with 100-iteration random search per method per dataset: a non-neural method takes best MAP@20 on 7 of 8 datasets `[V]`. RETAIL: STAN 0.0285 MAP@20 vs best neural (gru4rec) 0.0272. DIGI: sknn 0.0255 vs gru4rec 0.0247. ZALANDO: vstan 0.0168 vs sr-gnn 0.0146. The single exception is RSC15/Yoochoose, which the authors themselves flag as atypical `[V]`. Shehzad and Jannach (2023) repeated this against 8 GNNs and a simple method won MRR@20 on all four dataset configurations `[V]`.

The decisive point is not that kNN wins. It is that **CORE, MiaSRec, Mamba4Rec and SPRINT have never been benchmarked against STAN or VSTAN in any paper located.** Their published leads are over neural baselines only. Every claim of progress in session-based recommendation since 2022 is measured against models that themselves lose to session-KNN. Build VSTAN first, treat its number as the bar, and require every learned model to clear it on your own data. Most teams skip this and never learn that their transformer is worse than a 14ms lookup with no training phase.

**Finding 2: Your 30ms budget is consumed by the encoder, not by nearest-neighbour search. Do not buy a vector database.**

At 100k items x 768 dims, exact brute-force flat scan is roughly 0.42ms `[V, derived from a non-peer-reviewed but method-documented benchmark: Faiss 1.14.3, Xeon Gold 6230, 10M x 768d, Flat p50 42.3ms, linear in N]`. Elastic's independently published figure of ~8ns per 1024d float32 dot product on AVX-512 gives ~0.8ms for a 100k exact scan on one core. Vendor defaults agree: Weaviate converts flat to HNSW at 10,000 objects; Qdrant's `full_scan_threshold` defaults to the equivalent of 10,000 256-dim vectors; Lucene abandons graph traversal for brute force when the filtered doc count is below `num_candidates`.

Adopting HNSW at your scale buys 4.5x memory, 64x build time, tombstoned deletes, index staleness on a churning catalog, 3.6 points of recall loss, and a documented filtered-query failure mode (ACORN exists because filtered HNSW is broken enough to justify a SIGMOD paper: 2-1,000x throughput gains purely from fixing it `[V]`), in exchange for saving a fraction of one millisecond. A roaring-bitmap prefilter plus a flat scan is exact and gets **faster** as facet selectivity increases. Retrieval is a near-free layer for you. Spend the engineering elsewhere.

**Finding 3: No LLM belongs in the request path. Budget the LLM as a batch job that emits artifacts.**

The best-engineered LLM reranker in the literature, LlamaRec, reports inference "consistently under 1s" as its headline efficiency win `[U on the exact figure, but the qualitative claim is the paper's own framing]`. E4SRec, architecturally the fastest possible LLM design (frozen 13B, single forward pass, no autoregressive decode), measures 0.23s per instance on datacenter hardware `[U]`. Stack every 2025-2026 acceleration result (EARN 3.79x with 80.8% KV-cache reduction `[V]`, speculative decoding 3-5x `[C: survey said 2-2.5x]`, SLMRec 8.0x by pruning 28 of 32 layers `[V]`) and a 7B-class model is still an order of magnitude short, with a GPU per PoP.

The productive pattern is LEARN's: a frozen 7B LLM as an **offline item encoder** only, producing an embedding table that is looked up at serve time, with zero online LLM inference. SLMRec's null result is the argument that clinches it: pruning LLaMA-7B from 32 layers to 4 loses essentially nothing on recommendation accuracy `[V on the 13%-of-parameters and 8.0x claims]`. If 87% of the language model is redundant for the task, the language model was never where the signal lived.

**Finding 4: The bandit and logging layer, not the sequence model, is where anonymous lift comes from, and propensity logging is a one-way door.**

LinUCB at Yahoo is the only published production result whose evaluation **is** the anonymous case: request-time features, no user embedding, no per-user parameter, +12.5% clicks over a context-free bandit on 33M events `[V, but C: the 12.5% figure belongs to DISJOINT LinUCB; hybrid gets 8.4% at 100% data. The +20.1% at 1% data is hybrid and is correctly attributed]`. That "advantage grows as data gets scarcer" property is exactly your regime.

The irreversible decision is not which bandit. It is whether you log the propensity of the served action and reserve a 1-5% randomized exploration slice from day one. Without both, none of the off-policy evaluation stack (SNIPS, DR with shrinkage, MIPS) can ever run, and at 1k-100k items plain IPS is statistically dead anyway: IPS variance inflates by over 300x going from 10 to 5000 actions, while MIPS achieves 12.38x better MSE at |A|=5000 `[V]`. Retrofitting propensities onto logs that lack them is impossible. Teams that skip this end up with logs from which nothing can be learned.

**Finding 5: Expect sub-1% to low-single-digit lift, price your latency in revenue, and run the heterogeneity precheck before building anything.**

Booking.com measured that a 30% increase in prediction serving latency cost ~0.5% conversion `[U on the exact pairing; widely cited from the KDD 2019 paper]`. On that exchange rate, a session transformer that adds 10ms to a 30ms budget costs roughly half a percent of conversion before it delivers anything. BST's own published delta is 13ms to 20ms for +7.57% **relative** CTR `[C: the survey called this "+7.6% absolute", which would be implausible]`.

Meanwhile modern production lifts are small: OneRec-V2 +0.467%/+0.741% App Stay Time `[V]`; UniPinRec ~+1% engagement with an 11.1% latency reduction `[V]`; RLUR at Kuaishou converged after 100 days of full deployment at +0.2% DAU and +0.053% day-1 retention `[V]`; Dream11's contextual bandit ~0.4% revenue in A/B plus a further ~0.5% post-deployment `[C: survey reported "+0.51% (p=0.034)", which matches neither figure and whose p-value could not be located]`. These are statistically undetectable below roughly 10M sessions.

Before committing: run the actionable-heterogeneity check (Shchetkina, Wharton). Only **crossover** interactions are actionable, and applied to two large vaccination mega-experiments the framework attributed 18% vs 4% realizable personalization gain almost entirely to within-treatment heterogeneity, not to method choice `[V, but note the arXiv version was withdrawn for licensing reasons; cite the working paper]`. If your session-observable covariates show no crossover, the correct amount of personalization is zero and no architecture fixes that.

## 2. Ranked shortlist by layer

Data-scale thresholds are stated in **sessions available for training/calibration**. Where a published threshold exists it is cited; otherwise the threshold is an engineering judgment anchored to the dataset sizes on which the method was actually validated, and is labelled as such.

### 2.1 Session encoder

| Rank | Model | When to use | Threshold below which it is not worth it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **VSTAN / STAN (session-KNN)** | Default and baseline. No training phase, so new items enter the index the moment they appear in a session. This is the production system until something beats it on your data. | ~10k sessions in the recency window. Below that, fall back to item-to-item co-occurrence plus popularity. (Judgment; validated on datasets of 200k-1M+ sessions.) |
| 2 | **Item-to-item co-occurrence (Amazon-style)** | Day one, before any model exists. 100k x ~50 neighbour table is tens of MB, sub-1ms lookup, explicit popularity fallback for a zero-event visitor. | None. Works from the first day of logs. Note: Amazon published no A/B lift for the original system `[V]`. |
| 3 | **STAMP-style attention over last ~15 clicks (Zalando ZSF-c)** | The closest published match to this exact operating point: 80 items selected from ~120k at 500rps within 20ms p99, +6.23% CTR and +3.24% products ordered `[V]`. Initialize item embeddings from content/image vectors. | ~1M sessions. Zalando's short-term branch is the anonymous-usable half; the 5-purchase long-term branch is separable and must be dropped. |
| 4 | **CORE-ave** | Strongest accuracy-per-millisecond learned candidate. Session embedding is a weighted mean of item embeddings; scoring is one GEMM against the item table. Trivially inside 30ms at 100k items. | ~1M sessions. Published Recall@20 leads over SR-GNN of 4-11 points on five datasets, **but never benchmarked against STAN/VSTAN**. Treat its advantage over kNN as unmeasured. |
| 5 | **GRU4Rec (official Theano or official PyTorch only)** | If you want a recurrent state you can update one step per event and hold server-side. RecSys 2025 found a properly tuned GRU4Rec is best on 2 of 3 datasets against six 2020-2024 GNNs `[V on the paper's abstract; C: the survey's "beat every GNN on MRR, lost 3 on Hit Rate" sentence is copied from the different 2023 paper]`. Highest catalog coverage and lowest popularity bias of any model in Ludewig et al. | ~1M sessions. Hard constraint: third-party ports lose up to 99.63% of official performance `[C: the survey's "median 7% to 99%" range is not in the paper; best-case ports lose 0-4% on some datasets]`. Using a reimplementation voids the result. |
| 6 | **BST (single transformer encoder layer, candidate appended as last position)** | Only if a transformer serving path already exists. Published cost is the most useful latency datapoint in the field: 13ms to 20ms for +7.57% relative online CTR `[C]`. | ~5M sessions, and only after VSTAN has been beaten. |

**Downranked at this layer:** BERT4Rec (bidirectional cloze training presumes access to the full sequence including future events, which an in-flight session structurally lacks; needs up to 30x its default training budget to reproduce its own numbers `[V]`; slowest transformer at inference at 0.90s eval batch vs SASRec 0.27s `[V]`). All GNN session recommenders (see §3). Mamba4Rec (accuracy edge is ~5% relative over SASRec and flips sign on Amazon-Beauty where SASRec wins HR@10 0.0847 vs 0.0812 `[V]`; requires a selective-scan CUDA kernel in the serving stack; the long-sequence benefit you are paying for does not exist at 5-30 events). MiaSRec (gains concentrate on long-session datasets: Tmall +24.6% Recall@20 but Diginetica, at 4.8 events per session, only +1.2% over CORE, which is noise-band).

### 2.2 Item representation

This layer is content-side and works at **zero sessions**. That is its whole point: it is the cold-start and long-tail fix, and it is independent of identity because text embedding models embed items, never users.

| Rank | Model | When to use | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **EmbeddingGemma-300m at 256 dims, int8** | The only model with a published per-dimension truncation table you can size a tradeoff against: MTEB English v2 69.67 / 69.18 / 68.37 / 66.66 at 768/512/256/128; multilingual 61.15 / 60.71 / 59.68 / 58.23; code 68.76 / 68.48 / 66.74 / 62.96 `[V, exact match to the official model card]`. 768→256 costs 1.9% English. 100k items x 256 dims x int8 = 25MB, edge-resident, brute-force scannable. Ships QAT Q8_0/Q4_0 checkpoints. | Zero sessions. Constraint is 2K context, so long portal documents need chunking. `[U: the "<200MB RAM, <15-22ms on EdgeTPU" figures are a Google blog claim, not on the model card.]` |
| 2 | **Granite Embedding Multilingual R2 (97m / 311m)** | Cleanest license story (Apache 2.0) and the only entry with published throughput on named hardware: ~2,500 docs/sec (97m) and ~1,800 docs/sec (311m) on a single H100; MTEB Multilingual Retrieval 65.2 (311m) and 60.3 (97m); 768→256 costs 0.5 points, 128 dims retains >97% `[V, full spec sheet confirmed]`. | Zero sessions. Asymmetry that matters: only the 311m supports Matryoshka. The 97m is **fixed** at 384 dims. |
| 3 | **Offline encode with Qwen3-Embedding-4B/8B or KaLM-Gemma3-12B, ship truncated int8** | If you never encode free text at request time, pick on quality alone and encode the catalog offline on a GPU. KaLM-12B tops MMTEB at 72.32 with MRL down to 64 dims `[V]`; Qwen3-8B at 70.58, 0.6B at 64.64 `[C: survey said 64.34]`. Request-time cost is zero. | Zero sessions. KaLM-12B is 11.76B params / ~44.9GB: offline encoder only, never in the path. |
| 4 | **Arctic-Embed 2.0 medium (113M)** | When compression resilience matters more than headline quality. MRL loss applied at 256 dims during **both** pretraining and finetuning, paired with int4 to reach 128 bytes per document (100k items = 12.8MB). | Zero sessions. `[U: the truncation ablation table (MTEB-R 0.554→0.549 at 256 dims, competitors at 94%/93%) could not be verified. This is the survey's cleanest evidence for the model and it is unconfirmed.]` |
| 5 | **LEARN pattern: frozen LLM as offline item encoder** | The item-side half of Kuaishou's LEARN transfers directly: LLM text embeddings projected into the collaborative space by a trainable adapter, LLM never in the serving path. Reported +8.77% revenue on cold-start items and +5.79% on long-tail `[U]`. Use as an item cold-start fix, not a user model. | ~1M interactions to train the adapter. |
| 6 | **Semantic IDs via residual K-means (not RQ-VAE)** | If you go the generative-retrieval route. Spotify tried RQ-VAE in production and switched: R-KMeans gave better intra-bucket semantic consistency and higher HR@30, citing RQ-VAE codebook collapse, initialization sensitivity, and dependence on tuned auxiliary losses as "hard to deploy reliably" `[U on the +9.52% figure and the 0.856 vs 0.657 consistency numbers]`. | ~150k interactions (the entire TIGER/RPG literature trains at 148k-1.02M interactions on 12k-64k catalogs). |

**Excluded at this layer:**

| Model | Reason |
|---|---|
| jina-embeddings-v5-text (nano / small) | **CC BY-NC 4.0.** `[C: the survey flagged v4's non-commercial license as a blocker, then recommended v5 as the safe replacement and closed by recommending jina-v5-nano for deployment. Both v5 variants carry the same blocker.]` Scores are genuinely excellent (small: 71.7 MTEB English v2 at 677M) and irrelevant if you cannot ship it. |
| jina-embeddings-v4 | Non-commercial license. Multi-vector mode also requires 128 dims per token plus late-interaction scoring at query time, blowing both storage and latency budgets. |
| BGE-M3 | Not MRL-trained. FlagEmbedding raises an explicit error if you pass a `dimensions` parameter. You get 1024 dims or nothing: 100k items = 410MB float / 102MB int8, an order of magnitude worse than a truncatable model. Now scores 59.56 on MTEB v2 multilingual, below Qwen3-0.6B. |
| Harrier-OSS-v1 | MIT-licensed and tops MTEB v2 multilingual (27b: 74.3; 270m: 66.5) `[V]`, but **no MRL documented on any of the three model cards**. Fixed 640/1024 dims. Reopened as a candidate only if you do PCA yourself (see §3, item 4). |
| Cohere Embed v4, Gemini Embedding 2, voyage-3-large | API-only. Every live encode is a network round trip, which is structurally incompatible with a 30ms edge budget. Usable only in the offline-encode pattern. |

### 2.3 Retrieval

Thresholds here are in **catalog items**, not sessions.

| Rank | Method | When to use | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Exact flat scan (int8 or float32) with roaring-bitmap prefilter** | Default. Exact, zero build step, zero staleness, single-row updates with no reindex, and strictly faster as filters tighten. 100k x 768d = 307MB float32 / 77MB int8. | Correct up to ~1M items (flat at 1M ≈ 4.2ms). Becomes wrong somewhere between 1M and 10M (flat at 10M = 42.3ms p50, over budget). |
| 2 | **RRF fusion (lexical + dense + session-recency lists)** | The cheapest place in the entire system to add or remove a session signal: fusion runs over ranked lists at request time, so a pure session signal enters with zero retraining and zero cold-start. Fusion itself is microseconds. | Any scale. Be honest about magnitude: measured gains are +7.4% nDCG on WANDS e-commerce and +0.033 mean nDCG@10 in a 2026 RAG benchmark, not the 15-30% often claimed. |
| 3 | **uniCOIL with pre-computed query weights** | The learned-sparse configuration that does **zero neural work at request time**: MRR@10 0.3516 on MS MARCO with pre-computed query weights vs 0.3509 with on-the-fly encoding. Standard inverted index. | Any scale. Document-side BERT inference is a one-time offline cost. |
| 4 | **SPLADE-v3-Doc / SPLADE-v3-Lexical** | If you want learned sparse quality without paying for a query encoder. Full SPLADE-v3 hits 40.2 MRR@10 `[V]` but that assumes a query-side BERT forward pass, which on CPU **is** your 30ms budget. Doc variant: 37.8 MRR@10, 47.0 BEIR-13. | Any scale. Check the naver license terms (research/non-commercial). |
| 5 | **MUVERA Fixed Dimensional Encodings** | Converts late-interaction similarity into a single-vector inner product with approximation guarantees, so you get MaxSim-quality retrieval through a flat scan. Reports 10% improved recall with 90% lower latency vs PLAID, retrieving 2-5x fewer candidates `[C: survey said 5-20x]`. | Any scale. The most promising single combination for these constraints. |
| 6 | **HNSW / IVF-PQ / ScaNN / DiskANN / ACORN** | Only above ~1M vectors. DiskANN's headline result is 1 billion points on one machine; your entire corpus is 307MB of RAM. | Do not deploy below 1M items. |

**Excluded:** ColBERTv2/PLAID (101.3ms on 8 CPU cores at k=1000, over 3x budget; a reproducibility study found that simply reranking BM25 results with ColBERTv2 beats PLAID's efficiency-effectiveness tradeoff in low-latency settings, which is exactly your regime `[V on the paper's existence and framing]`). WARP (2-4x smaller index and 41x over XTR's reference implementation `[V]`, but the absolute latencies that make it interesting are `[U]`; see §5).

### 2.4 Ranking / policy

| Rank | Model | When to use | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Vowpal Wabbit contextual bandit stack (SquareCB or epsilon-greedy) over sections/rankers** | The most deployable option in this survey and the only one that meets 30ms with zero architectural gymnastics: microseconds per prediction, feature hashing so you never allocate a per-user parameter, propensity logging and replay OPE built in. Reported >25% CTR on the MSN homepage Slate segment and +5.3% on the 10x-traffic Panel segment `[V]`. | ~50k sessions to see a signal on a ~10-50 arm problem. Rule of thumb: you need roughly 10^3 reward events per arm-by-context cell. |
| 2 | **Linear Thompson Sampling (LinTS) over a 100-500 candidate shortlist** | Best serving economics of any method here. LinTS draws **one** parameter sample per request and scores every candidate with a single dot product: O(K·d). LinUCB needs a per-item quadratic form: O(K·d²), roughly an order of magnitude more at the same d. At d=64, K=500 this is 1-3ms. | ~100k sessions. Disjoint LinUCB memory is K·d² floats: at 100k items and d=64 that is ~1.6GB, so a retrieval shortlist is mandatory, not optional. |
| 3 | **Cascading bandits (CascadeKL-UCB)** | The correct statistical treatment of what a portal actually shows: a ranked list where a click at position 3 is informative about positions 1-2. No user parameter at all in the base formulation. KL-UCB variant is specifically stronger when attraction probabilities are low, which is the portal regime. O(N log K), 100k items in well under 1ms. | ~100k sessions. The real cost is maintaining the position/examination model, not the bandit. |
| 4 | **NeuralLinear Thompson Sampling** | The neural upgrade path if linear runs out of expressiveness. One forward pass on the session context, then linear-cost sampling over candidates. The ICLR 2018 Deep Bayesian Bandits Showdown found methods that succeed in supervised learning **underperform** in the bandit loop, and NeuralLinear is the robust winner `[V]`. | ~1M sessions. Watch for catastrophic forgetting in the linear head when the trunk is retrained. |
| 5 | **RCR (regression-compatible listwise loss)** | Highest ROI item in the whole survey for a tight budget: it is a **training-objective change with literally zero added inference cost** and no post-hoc calibration table to keep fresh. Fixes the fact that listwise softmax losses are scale-invariant, so your top score is not a probability. YouTube Search: +0.66% SearchCTR, -0.31% abandoned rate; Yahoo NDCG@10 0.6852→0.7050 while LogLoss **improves** 0.4296→0.4187 `[V]`. | ~1M labeled impressions. Do this before any post-hoc calibrator. |
| 6 | **AdaCalib or MCNet (field-aware post-hoc calibration)** | When you cannot change the training loss. Fields are context (slot, source, entry page, dwell bucket), never identity. AdaCalib online A/B: +5.05% CVR, +5.47% GMV against smoothed isotonic `[V]`. MCNet fixed a base model from PCOC 0.7727 to 0.9745 with AUC unchanged to the fourth decimal (0.7216→0.7215) `[V]`, which is the operational fact that makes a calibrated confidence gate affordable. | ~1M impressions per calibration group. |
| 7 | **SlateQ decomposition (the idea, not the system)** | Useful modelling structure for in-session slate construction: single-choice plus reward-depends-only-on-consumed-item turns an intractable combinatorial Q into per-item values plus greedy top-k. Use greedy top-k, not the LP. | Do not expect RL-scale gains. With episodic reset there is almost no long-horizon value to capture, so the gap to a greedy slate bandit shrinks toward zero. |

**OPE stack, in adoption order:** (1) log propensities from day one, non-negotiable; (2) SNIPS as the default point estimate; (3) DR with optimistic shrinkage (DRos) once a reward model exists; (4) **MIPS as soon as your action space exceeds a few hundred items** (1.38x better MSE than IPS at |A|=10 rising to 12.38x at |A|=5000, against an IPS variance inflation of >300x over the same range `[V]`), using an item embedding you already have (taxonomy, department, document type) with SLOPE for dimension selection; (5) the pseudoinverse estimator if you serve slates rather than single items.

`[U]` beta*-IPS is presented in the survey as making SNIPS "provably obsolete." The RecSys 2024 paper and its unifying-framework claim are `[V]`, but the specific MAE figures (0.1078 vs SNIPS 0.1113) and the SIGIR 2026 follow-up on which the dominance proof rests are unconfirmed. Adopt it if you like (it is a closed-form scalar computed from quantities you already log, so it costs nothing), but do not rewrite your evaluation plan around a claim that could not be sourced.

### 2.5 Uncertainty

| Rank | Model | When to use | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Score calibration (RCR at training time, else isotonic/MCNet post-hoc)** | Prerequisite for every gating decision downstream. Measure ECE and PCOC before doing anything cleverer. This is also the only sub-layer here with real production numbers attached, and none of them need a user ID. | ~1M impressions. |
| 2 | **Split conformal / Conformal Risk Control over sessions** | Structurally the friendliest UQ family to this problem, and nobody says so. The exchangeability unit is the **calibration example**, and you choose what that is: make it one session or one ranking request and the theory holds verbatim with zero persistent identity. Deployed artifact is a single scalar lambda-hat. Serving cost is a comparison against a sort you already do. Guarantee is alpha - 2B/(n+1) ≤ E[L] ≤ alpha `[U on the empirical tightness figures]`. | ~2k-5k calibration sessions, on a **rolling window**. Static lambda-hat shipped once is a guarantee in name only: portal traffic is bursty and breaks exchangeability. Pair with adaptive conformal inference (Gibbs and Candes, NeurIPS 2021). |
| 3 | **RAPS set size as the personalize/don't-personalize gate** | This is the deliverable, not the set. Wide set means the session has not disambiguated intent: fall back to editorial/popular. Narrow set: commit. Do **not** render the set: guaranteed sets in ranking are large (68 documents on MSLR-Web10K at alpha=0.1, 27 on Yahoo LTRC). | ~2k calibration sessions. Two hyperparameters (k_reg, lambda) tuned on a small holdout. |
| 4 | **CPFT (conformal-aware fine-tuning)** | If a session model already exists. Adds a differentiable set-size proxy to the training objective; **zero added inference latency**, since inference is identical to the unmodified SASRec/UniSRec backbone. Reported +4.551% average across metrics and set size falling from 48 to 27 items `[U]`. All four evaluated backbones are item-sequence models with no user-ID embedding, so they are natively anonymous. | ~200k sessions (evaluated on five Amazon datasets). |

**Excluded at this layer:** deep ensembles and MC-dropout (M models or T forward passes; the arithmetic does not work at 30ms over 1k-100k items). LiDu (conceptually exactly the "should I trust this?" gate you want, and label-free, but its best variant tops out at Pearson r = -0.222 with actual list performance `[V]`, which is a legitimate research result and not a foundation for a binary production switch). SelectiveNet (improvement over thresholding a calibrated softmax is real but modest, 2.43% vs 2.89% selective risk at 90% coverage on CIFAR-10 `[U]`, and it requires a separately trained model per coverage target, so a coverage sweep is N models rather than one knob).

**Naming trap that costs teams weeks:** "calibrated recommendation" is a name collision. Steck-style calibration means matching the genre/content-type **distribution** of a slate to the user's historical consumption mix via KL divergence. It has nothing to do with probability calibration, ECE, or confidence. Spotify's impressive deployed result in that second sense (+36.6% podcast impression-to-stream, +3.93% overall `[V]`) explicitly requires "past consumption behavior across content types aggregated over various time windows," which is a per-user profile and useless to you.

## 3. Overhyped, does not reproduce, or needs data you lack

| Claim / method | What the evidence actually says |
|---|---|
| **GNN session recommenders as a class** | Dozens of variants 2019-2024, each claiming to beat the last. Under identical tuning, a simple method won MRR@20 on **every** dataset configuration against 8 GNNs `[V]`; several GNNs land more than 50% below the best MRR despite claiming state of the art. RecSys 2025: only the early SR-GNN was superior on one of three datasets, and GNN performance varies largely across datasets `[V]`. That the field was still publishing a fix for "the session representation collapses to the last item" at WWW 2026 is itself the verdict. |
| **GCE-GNN specifically** | Published as a clear winner over SR-GNN on three datasets. Independent reproduction: **last** of 12 on RETAIL (MRR@20 0.429 vs VSTAN 0.631), last on RSC15-1/12 (0.225 vs SR 0.344), second-last on DIGI `[V]`. Cost: 1,153.9 minutes (~19 hours) to train once and **503.5ms per prediction**, roughly 17x over your budget `[V]`. `[C: the survey also called it the most popularity-biased GNN; that is TAGNN at Pop@20 0.102 vs GCE-GNN 0.097.]` |
| **COTREC** | ~2 seconds per recommendation list `[V]`. The reproduction authors call this prohibitive in practice. They are right. |
| **BERT4Rec as a session model** | Needs up to 30x its default training budget to reproduce its own published numbers `[V]`. Conceptually mismatched: bidirectional cloze training presumes access to the full sequence including future events; at serving time the mask is always the last position, so you paid a bidirectional training bill to end up with a causal model. Also the slowest transformer at inference in every measurement located. |
| **Published "we beat GRU4Rec" claims** | Third-party GRU4Rec ports lose up to **99.63%** of the official implementation's performance out of the box `[C: the survey's "median 7% to 99%" statistic does not exist in the paper; the word "median" never appears, and best-case ports lose 0-4% on some datasets]`. Since GRU4Rec is the near-universal baseline, a large fraction of the literature is benchmarking against a crippled port. Combined with "Everyone's a Winner!" (tuning effort determines the winner) `[V]`, treat every published leaderboard delta as unverified until you re-run both arms yourself. |
| **Sampled-negative evaluation** | SASRec, BERT4Rec, TiSASRec and Mamba4Rec all rank the target against 100 sampled negatives rather than the full catalog. That protocol is known to distort model rankings and is not the task you face at serve time. |
| **Semantic-ID tokenizer comparisons** | The standard protocol checks whether the target's SID sequence appears in the beam, which silently equates SID-level matching with item-level recommendation. Across four datasets and five tokenizers, up to **30.5%** of items share an SID with at least one other item, and Hit@10 is inflated by up to **103.36%** `[V]`, enough to flip pairwise tokenizer comparisons. Every "tokenizer X beats Y by N%" claim in this literature is currently unverified. |
| **RQ-VAE as the default tokenizer** | Most-cited component, first one production teams drop. Spotify switched to residual K-means citing codebook collapse, initialization sensitivity, and dependence on tuned auxiliary losses. Start with residual K-means; treat RQ-VAE as an optional experiment. |
| **"Scale the SID recommender and it improves"** | Measured and refuted. A 44M-to-14B sweep across all three axes found performance saturates on every one, with enlarging the modality encoder giving negligible-to-zero gain, and LLM-as-RS beating the best achievable SID-based GR by up to 20% `[V on the sweep and the 20% figure; U on the specific 13M saturation point, which sits below the sweep's stated 44M floor]`. |
| **TIGER's cold-start generalization** | Marketed as a headline benefit. A 2026 reproducibility study under unified cold-start protocols found TIGER's cold-start performance **near zero**, with hybrid models like LIGER needed to recover it. The mechanism is obvious in hindsight: the decoder can only emit SID sequences it was trained to emit. On a churning portal catalog you hit this in week two, and the fix is a dense retrieval fallback, not a better tokenizer. |
| **Beam search as a free lunch** | The hidden latency cost in every TIGER port. Replacing beam search with greedy decoding costs Recall, and Spotify ended at beam 30, rebuilt CPU request orchestration for an 8x throughput gain, and serves event-triggered-and-cached rather than synchronously `[U on the specific -27.07% and 8x figures]`. A synchronous 30ms budget with an autoregressive SID decoder is a real engineering risk. Use RPG's parallel decode (avg +12.6% NDCG@10 over generative baselines `[V]`) or SID-MLP distillation (8.74x speedup at teacher accuracy `[V]`). |
| **"Generative recommenders need Meta-scale data"** | This one is wrong in the **other** direction, for the semantic-ID family. TIGER/RPG/ActionPiece/LIGER are trained and evaluated on 148k-1.02M interactions with 12k-64k item catalogs and ~9-event sequences at ~13M parameters. That regime is smaller than most portals. The scale requirement is real only for HSTU, OneRec, PLUM and Netflix-class systems, which is exactly the set whose gains depend on identity you do not have. |
| **Zero-shot / in-context LLM ranking** | The loudest claim, the weakest evidence. LLMRank's own numbers put a frontier model ~30 NDCG points below a small trained SASRec on ML-1M `[U on the specific values, and note they are over a 20-candidate reranking task, not full-catalog ranking]`. Plus documented position bias (ground truth at candidate position 19 ranks far worse than at position 0) whose mitigation triples the cost. |
| **"LLMs understand user sequences"** | "Lost in Sequence" (KDD 2025) shuffled the interaction order and found existing LLM4Rec models barely degrade: they are reading the trajectory as a bag. For a 5-30 event session where order is nearly the only signal you have, that is disqualifying. |
| **"Efficient LLM recommender" papers measured in seconds** | LlamaRec's <1s and E4SRec's 0.23s are efficiency wins relative to a 56s baseline. Against 30ms they are failures. Read every efficiency claim in this space as a multiplier on an already-impossible baseline. |
| **RL for anonymous short-session recommendation** | RLUR at Kuaishou is the honest published number: after 100 days of full deployment, +0.2% DAU, +0.053% day-1 retention `[V]`. That is a colossal program for five hundredths of a percent, and it is measurable only **because** Kuaishou can track the same user for 100 days. With episodic reset the long-horizon term these methods exist to optimize is approximately zero. |
| **CQL / IQL for recsys** | Essentially no published production recsys deployment with a clean A/B number. What exists is auto-bidding and notifications: single-agent, low-cardinality-action, long-horizon, i.e. the opposite of a 100k-item anonymous slate. Two survey papers make the same point: RL-recsys benchmarks are artificial or semi-simulated, so offline wins carry almost no information about online behaviour. |
| **NeuralUCB** | Theoretically beautiful, practically awkward. The confidence matrix is p x p in **network parameters**; the paper's own experiments fall back to a diagonal approximation that voids the theory, and the bonus requires a per-candidate backward pass. If you build one neural bandit, build NeuralLinear. |
| **ANN benchmarks and vector DB marketing** | ann-benchmarks charts are single-threaded, unfiltered, static-index, batch-friendly, and measured at 1M+ vectors. None of those conditions holds for a facet-heavy portal at 100k items with a live catalog. Google's own note that ann-benchmarks shows outdated numbers for ScaNN tells you the leaderboard is substantially a tuning contest. |
| **MTEB leaderboard position** | The 12B-27B tier is not deployable at 30ms and its advantage over a 239M model is ~3-5 points averaged over 131 tasks spanning bitext mining, code and instruction-following, almost none of which resemble item retrieval on a portal catalog. MTEB v1 and v2 scores are **not comparable** and vendors quote whichever flatters (which is why bge-m3 appears as both 68.2 and 59.56 in different 2026 comparisons). Build a 200-query golden set from your own portal. |
| **MRL as a required training property** | A controlled 2026 study found head-truncation robustness "is not unique to Matryoshka-compatible models... it appears to be a broader property of recent high-dimensional text embedding models," with Qwen3-Embedding behaving comparably to non-MRL models. An independent 8-dataset BEIR test found **PCA matched or beat MRL truncation at nearly every dimension for every model tested**. Practical consequence: you have a fixed catalog. Fit PCA on your own item vectors. This reopens Harrier-OSS-v1 and bge-m3 as candidates. |
| **Binary quantization "96% retained, 32x smaller"** | Two problems. It is model-dependent: on the same benchmark, one model retains 96.45% and another collapses to 74.77%. And the good number **requires a rescoring pass against float vectors**, so the advertised storage saving partly evaporates. The verifiable figures are float32 54.39 → int8 52.79 → binary 52.46 NDCG@10 on mxbai-embed-large-v1, i.e. 92.53% retention without rescoring and 96.45% with `[C: the survey's four point-deltas (-6.50/-1.95/-0.09/-0.93) are not present at the cited sbert.net URL nor in the HF blog it defers to]`. **int8 is the honest default:** ~99% retention, 4x smaller, 2.7-3.4x faster on CPU, no rescoring pass. |
| **"WebGPU is 100x faster than WASM"** | Category error. That is a batch-throughput figure. At batch 1, the case an interactive budget cares about, the confirmed measurement is all-MiniLM-L6-v2 at seq-len 512: **45.70ms WebGPU vs 507.20ms WASM, an 11.1x ratio** `[V on that single machine; C: the survey's batch-128 pair (1518ms vs 50,497ms) is not retrievable at the cited URL, which tops out at batch 32 at ~28x]`. The decision-relevant fact survives intact: one batch-1 in-browser transformer encode consumes your entire 30ms budget, before candidate scoring, plus a 23MB int8 download and shader compilation on first paint. |
| **WebNN, LiteRT.js, Chrome built-in AI** | WebNN: W3C Candidate Recommendation, Chrome origin trial only, nothing in Safari or Firefox, realistic default-path timeline 2027. LiteRT.js: "up to 3x" with **no millisecond figures published** `[V that no figures exist]`, which disqualifies it as a latency-critical decision input today. Gemini Nano: ~4GB weights gated on 22GB free disk and 4GB VRAM, Chrome-desktop-only, deletable by the browser. Every design that "uses the built-in model when available" needs a complete non-LLM fallback, which means the fallback is the actual system. |
| **Federated learning for anonymous portals** | Structurally inapplicable, and this is the most important negative result in the edge dimension. FL is a **training-time** technique requiring the same device to hold a stable local dataset and participate across many rounds. Gboard works because a keyboard has a persistent device with months of local text `[V on the deployment claims]`. A 5-30 event anonymous session gives it nothing to federate. The honest privacy story here is stronger and simpler: nothing leaves the device because the personalization state **is** the session and dies with the tab. |
| **Chrome Topics API as a personalization signal** | 469 human-curated topics, at most 3 per weekly epoch, Chrome-only. Three coarse topics per week cannot rank a 100k-item catalog: the taxonomy granularity is off by three orders of magnitude. It is also not a free privacy win: an independent assessment on 1,207 real browsing histories found cross-site re-identification probability of 2%/3%/4% after one/two/three observations, worsening over time `[V]`. Weak cold-start prior at most. |
| **Device-cloud collaborative recommendation papers** | Representative case: CHORD reports 61.2x-173.8x parameter-transmission reduction and +51.8% NDCG@10 on MovieLens-100K, which has 943 users and 928 items. That is a toy catalog three orders of magnitude below your range, with no on-device latency, no wall-clock timing, and no real-device deployment. Directionally interesting, numerically unusable. |
| **Elaborate embedding compression for recommenders** | A thorough benchmark found plain **magnitude pruning** outperformed the elaborate alternatives, that methods behaved very differently across collaborative-filtering vs content-based tasks (so results do not transfer between paradigms), and that running on a Raspberry Pi 4 exposed efficiency constraints the papers had not surfaced. Start with the dumbest compression that works. |
| **Offline session-benchmark wins generally** | Booking.com's central finding is that improving a model's offline metric frequently does not move business value, and it lands hardest on session-based recommendation. Thumbtack's diagnostic paper `[V on existence; U on the specific numbers]` reports offline-to-online rank correlation that was **anti-correlated** on a real deployment, and Direct Method error several times wider than the confidence interval it was reported with. Use OPE as a filter, never as an oracle. |

The rule: if the mechanism that produces the published lift is a per-user row, a user embedding table, or a multi-day trajectory, then truncating to an anonymous 5-30 event session deletes the mechanism. What remains is a plain sequence model with a worse constant factor.

## 6. Build order, tied to sessions available

Latency budget allocation to enforce at every stage: **retrieval ≤ 2ms, encode ≤ 5ms, policy ≤ 3ms, everything else is headroom.** If a component cannot state its p99 in milliseconds on your hardware, it is not a candidate.

### Stage 0: before any sessions exist (day 0, catalog only)

1. Encode the catalog offline with EmbeddingGemma-300m or Granite R2 311m. Truncate to 256 dims with **PCA fit on your own item vectors** (not head truncation, not MRL), then int8. 100k items = ~25MB, edge-resident.
2. Item-to-item content similarity table from those vectors, plus a lexical index (BM25 or uniCOIL with pre-computed query weights).
3. Serve: session vector = pooled item embeddings of the last 5-30 events, exact flat scan with a roaring-bitmap facet prefilter, RRF fusion of the dense list, the lexical list, and a recency list. Popularity fallback for a zero-event visitor.
4. **Instrument the logging contract now:** served action, full slate, propensity, context features, reward. Reserve a 1-5% randomized exploration slice. This is the one decision you cannot retrofit.
5. Run the actionable-heterogeneity precheck on whatever historical logs exist. If session-observable covariates show no crossover interaction, stop here and say so.

### Stage 1: ~10k-100k sessions

6. Build **VSTAN and STAN**. No training run, so this ships in days. Measure MAP@20, MRR@20, HR@20 and coverage against Stage 0 on a time-contiguous split (never a random split). Record the number. It is the bar for everything that follows.
7. Add a **contextual bandit over sections/rankers/modules**, not over raw items: VW with SquareCB or epsilon-greedy, context = referrer, entry page, device, locale, hour, and a recency-weighted in-session category histogram. Arms in the tens, not the thousands. This is where the Yahoo result lives.
8. Calibrate. Isotonic or Platt first, measure ECE and PCOC, then field-aware (AdaCalib/MCNet shape) keyed on **context** fields, never identity.
9. Stand up the OPE harness: SNIPS as the default estimate, replay evaluation on the randomized slice. Do not yet trust it as an oracle.

### Stage 2: ~100k-1M sessions

10. Add **cascading bandits** for slate assembly with an explicit position/examination model. A click at position 3 is information about positions 1-2, and treating it otherwise is a statistical error you are currently making.
11. Switch to **LinTS over a 100-500 candidate shortlist** if per-item scoring is warranted: O(K·d) per request, one Cholesky cached and refreshed on batch update.
12. Adopt **MIPS** for off-policy evaluation, using taxonomy/department/document-type as the action embedding, with SLOPE for dimension selection. At this action-space size plain IPS is statistically dead.
13. Calibrate **conformal risk control over sessions**: split conformal on a rolling window of 2k-5k recent sessions, RAPS-style adaptive nonconformity score. Ship the lambda-hat. Use **set size as the personalize/don't-personalize gate**, not as a UI element. Monitor realized coverage as a first-class metric; a static threshold is a guarantee in name only on bursty traffic.
14. Optional and cheap: switch the ranker's training objective to **RCR**. Zero added inference cost, no post-hoc table to keep fresh.

### Stage 3: ~1M-10M sessions

15. Only now train a learned session encoder, and only as a challenger to VSTAN under equal tuning budgets on the same time-contiguous splits. Order of attempts: **CORE-ave** (one weighted mean plus one GEMM, cheapest to serve), then **STAMP-style attention over the last ~15 events** (the Zalando configuration, closest published match to your operating point), then **official-implementation GRU4Rec**. Do not use a third-party GRU4Rec port.
16. If any challenger wins, hold the win: retest quarterly. If none wins, that is a result, not a failure, and it is the modal outcome in the independently-run literature.
17. Add an **offline LLM batch job** producing item artifacts only: enriched item text, semantic IDs via residual K-means, session-intent labels (the SPRINT pattern, with the LLM confined to training and a distilled lightweight predictor at inference), synthetic sessions for cold-start items. Nothing LLM-shaped enters the request path.
18. Consider **NeuralLinear TS** if the linear bandit has visibly saturated. Not NeuralUCB.
19. Consider **CPFT** to fine-tune the session encoder for tight conformal sets: training-time only, zero inference cost.

### Stage 4: >10M sessions

20. This is the first point at which a production A/B can detect a 0.5% effect, which is the realistic magnitude of everything remaining. Plan experiments accordingly, and expect months of runtime.
21. Semantic-ID generative retrieval becomes worth evaluating as a **retrieval leg alongside dense**, never as a replacement: **RPG** for its parallel decode (decode complexity independent of catalog size, avg +12.6% NDCG@10 over generative baselines) or SID-MLP distillation. Mandatory companion: a dense fallback (the LIGER pattern) because a decoder can only emit SIDs it was trained to emit, and your catalog churns. Evaluate at **item level, not SID level**, given that collisions inflate published Hit@10 by up to 103%.
22. Revisit ANN only if the catalog crosses ~1M items. Below that, exact flat scan remains correct and cheaper.
23. Do **not** revisit HSTU, OneRec, PLUM, lifelong sequence models, or RL. More sessions do not create persistent identity, and identity is what those methods monetize.
